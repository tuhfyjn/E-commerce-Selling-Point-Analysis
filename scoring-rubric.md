# 100-Point Scoring Rubric

## 1. Instant recognition — 15

- product identity is obvious
- product is visually dominant
- thumbnail remains readable

## 2. Hook strength — 15

- clear reason to stop/click
- understandable in 0.5–3 seconds
- relevant to user need

## 3. Purchase reason — 20

- goes beyond raw features
- maps to a meaningful user outcome
- answers “why buy this?”

## 4. Differentiation — 15

- not generic category copy
- meaningful contrast with common competitors
- unique value is memorable

## 5. Visual proof — 15

- visual supports the claim
- scene/comparison/detail/data is used effectively
- avoids relying only on text

## 6. Information hierarchy — 10

- one visual boss
- clear primary/secondary levels
- no clutter

## 7. Trust and compliance — 10

- claims are supportable
- certifications are real
- comparisons are fair
- no fabricated guarantees or absolute claims

## Interpretation

- 90–100: strong candidate for A/B testing
- 80–89: solid; optimize weaker dimensions
- 70–79: usable but not sharp enough
- 60–69: mostly product-description thinking
- below 60: rework strategy, not just design

## Revision rule

If total < 85:
1. identify the two lowest dimensions
2. revise only those first
3. rescore
4. repeat until ≥85 or evidence constraints prevent improvement
